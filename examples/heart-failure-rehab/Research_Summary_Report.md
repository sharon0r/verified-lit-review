# Literature Review Report (5 articles)

**Research question:** Does exercise-based cardiac rehabilitation reduce hospital readmissions in patients with heart failure?

**PICO:** Population: adults with heart failure (chronic HFrEF or HFpEF, or after hospitalisation for acute decompensated HF). Intervention: exercise-based cardiac rehabilitation (exercise training alone or as part of a comprehensive programme; centre-, home- or hybrid-based). Comparison: usual care with no formal exercise. Outcome: hospital readmission or admission (all-cause and HF-related).

> **What is verified:** every **quote** was automatically checked, word for word, against the text of the PDF page it is attributed to; unverified quotes were removed. **Findings, the conclusion and caveats** are an AI-written summary of the papers and are not machine-checked; verify them against the source before professional use.

> **Scope:** 5 open-access papers chosen by an AI agent from the search results. This is a rapid first look, not a systematic review: papers that are not open access, and some key reviews in the field, may be missing.

## Conclusion: what the literature says

Exercise-based cardiac rehabilitation probably reduces all-cause hospital admissions in the first 12 months for adults with heart failure. The 2024 Cochrane review (60 RCTs) found an RR of 0.69 (95% CI 0.56 to 0.86; moderate certainty). The effect on HF-specific admissions is uncertain (RR 0.82, 95% CI 0.49 to 1.35, with possible publication bias), and so is the long-term all-cause effect (RR 0.84, 95% CI 0.70 to 1.01, heterogeneous). Two smaller meta-analyses found significant reductions in rehospitalisation: one in older patients (OR 0.32) and one of early, in-hospital-started rehabilitation for acute decompensated HF (OR 0.67, 3 trials). However, they are smaller, of lower quality and probably share trials with the Cochrane review, so they are not independent confirmation. The size of the effect varies widely between analyses. Two studies point the other way. A 322-patient HFpEF RCT found no fewer all-cause or cardiovascular hospitalisations with 12 months of training (numerically more in the exercise group). A propensity-matched HFrEF cohort found no significant association with HF rehospitalisation. Still unknown: the effect on HF-specific readmission, the effect in HFpEF and in frail patients after acute decompensation, and how long any benefit lasts.

**Certainty of evidence (GRADE-style, approximate):** Moderate for all-cause hospital admission in the short term (up to 12 months), based mainly on the 2024 Cochrane review of RCTs. Downgraded one level for risk of bias (poorly reported randomisation, allocation concealment and blinding). For HF-specific admissions the certainty is lower than the Cochrane rating suggests: the pooled CI includes 1 (imprecision) and the funnel plot was asymmetric (possible publication bias), so low certainty is more appropriate. For long-term all-cause admissions, results were inconsistent (I2 = 61%) and the CI includes 1. Further indirectness: the largest HFpEF trial found no reduction, and few trials enrolled patients with acutely decompensated HF. This is an approximate rating based on 5 open-access papers. The large HF-ACTION trial could not be retrieved, although it is included in the Cochrane review.

## 1. Overall effect of exercise-based cardiac rehabilitation on all-cause and HF-related hospital admissions

**Title:** Exercise-based cardiac rehabilitation for adults with heart failure  
**Authors:** Molloy C, Long L, Mordi IR, Bridges C, Sagar VA, Davies EJ, Coats AJ, Dalal H  
**Source:** The Cochrane database of systematic reviews  
**Study type:** Cochrane systematic review and meta-analysis of 60 RCTs (8728 participants); median follow-up 6 months; short-term (≤12 months) and long-term (>12 months) analyses  
**Why chosen:** It is the most recent and most comprehensive synthesis of randomised evidence with GRADE ratings, and it addresses the question directly.  
**Found in:** PubMed · downloaded via: OpenAlex  
**DOI:** [10.1002/14651858.CD003331.pub6](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD003331.pub6)  
**File:** `10100214651858CD003331pub6_721714be.pdf`

### Key findings

- Short-term all-cause hospital admissions: 15.9% with exercise rehabilitation vs 23.8% with control (RR 0.69, 95% CI 0.56 to 0.86; 23 trials, 2283 participants; moderate certainty, downgraded for risk of bias).
- HF-related admissions in the short term: RR 0.82 (95% CI 0.49 to 1.35; 10 trials, 911 participants). The CI includes 1, so the reduction is not statistically significant, although the authors call it a likely reduction (moderate certainty).
- Long-term all-cause admissions: RR 0.84 (95% CI 0.70 to 1.01; I2 = 61%; 7 trials, 3509 participants). This is not statistically significant and is heterogeneous.
- Effects appeared consistent across centre- and home-based delivery and across exercise-only and comprehensive programmes. Funnel-plot asymmetry was found for HF-related admissions (Egger P = 0.015).

**Relevance and caveats:** This review answers the question directly with RCT data. Limits: most trials enrolled HFrEF patients in NYHA class II–III, and only two trials enrolled patients with acute decompensated HF. Many trials reported randomisation and allocation concealment poorly. The authors' wording 'likely reduces' HF admissions should be read alongside a CI that includes 1 and possible publication bias. Most primary trials in the other meta-analyses here are probably also included in this review.

## 2. Cardiac rehabilitation and readmission in elderly patients (≥60 years) with chronic heart failure

**Title:** Effects of cardiac rehabilitation on elderly patients with Chronic heart failure: A meta-analysis and systematic review  
**Authors:** Chen Z, Li M, Yin C, Fang Y, Zhu Y, Feng J  
**Source:** PloS one  
**Study type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of 16 RCTs (1782 patients). The readmission outcome came from 4 RCTs (214 intervention, 224 control); fixed-effect model  
**Why chosen:** It focuses on older patients, a group under-represented in the Cochrane trials, and it reports readmission specifically.  
**Found in:** PubMed · downloaded via: PMC open-data (AWS)  
**DOI:** [10.1371/journal.pone.0273251](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0273251)  
**File:** `101371journalpone0273251_286ba577.pdf`

### Key findings

- Cardiac rehabilitation was associated with a lower readmission rate than usual care (OR 0.32, 95% CI 0.21 to 0.49; I2 = 0%).
- Only 4 of the 16 trials reported readmission.
- The authors state that most included samples were small and of low quality; only 3 studies reported allocation concealment.

**Relevance and caveats:** This review gives direct evidence in older patients, but it is based on only 4 small trials of limited quality. Its effect estimate (OR 0.32) is much larger than the Cochrane RR of 0.69. The difference may reflect small-study effects, different metrics (OR vs RR) and the selected trials, so the estimate is probably inflated. Readmission is not defined by cause, and the trials may overlap with the Cochrane review.

> "The readmission rate was calculated for 214 patients in the experimental group and 224 patients in the control group."

## 3. Early exercise-based rehabilitation started during hospitalisation for acute decompensated heart failure

**Title:** Early Exercise-Based Rehabilitation for Patients with Acute Decompensated Heart Failure: A Systemic Review and Meta-Analysis  
**Authors:** Liu L, Chen J, Zhao N, Zhang M, Zhou L, Ren X, Zhang T, Zhao P  
**Source:** Reviews in cardiovascular medicine  
**Study type:** Systematic review and meta-analysis of 6 RCTs (668 patients). All-cause rehospitalisation came from 3 trials; follow-up about 3–6 months  
**Why chosen:** It is the only synthesis found that covers the post-acute (readmission-prone) population, starting rehabilitation during the index admission.  
**Found in:** PubMed · downloaded via: PMC open-data (AWS)  
**DOI:** [10.31083/j.rcm2311356](https://doi.org/10.31083/j.rcm2311356)  
**File:** `1031083jrcm2311356_e42c2205.pdf`

### Key findings

- All-cause rehospitalisation was lower with early rehabilitation (OR 0.67, 95% CI 0.45 to 0.99, p = 0.04; 3 trials, I2 = 0%).
- Exercise capacity improved (6-minute walk distance MD 32.97, 95% CI 31.03 to 34.90).
- Falls or injuries did not differ significantly between groups (OR 0.68, 95% CI 0.43 to 1.08).

**Relevance and caveats:** This review is directly relevant to readmission after an acute HF hospitalisation. However, the rehospitalisation result rests on 3 trials, mostly the 349-patient REHAB-HF trial (Kitzman 2021), and the upper CI bound is close to 1. Both REHAB-HF and Reeves 2017 are also in the Cochrane review, so this is not independent confirmation. The interventions were multidomain physical rehabilitation for older, frail patients, which differs from standard outpatient cardiac rehabilitation. The safety analysis rests on few events.

### Quotes from the article

> "The rate of all-cause rehospitalization was significantly decreased in the early rehabilitation group (OR: 0.67, 95% CI: 0.45 to 0.99, p = 0.04)."

## 4. Combined endurance and resistance training in HFpEF: hospitalisations as safety and secondary outcomes

**Title:** Combined endurance and resistance exercise training in heart failure with preserved ejection fraction: a randomized controlled trial  
**Authors:** Edelmann F, Wachter R, Duvinage A, Mueller S, Fegers-Wustrow I, Schwarz S, Christle JW, Pieske-Kraigher E, Seyfarth M, Knapp M, Dörr M, Nolte K, Düngen HD, Herrmann-Lingen C, Esefeld K, Hagendorff A, Haykowsky MJ, Hasenfuss G, Holzendorf V, Prettin C, Mende M, Pieske B, Halle M.  
**Source:** Nature medicine  
**Study type:** Multicentre RCT, 322 patients with HFpEF (mean age 70, 59.6% female), 12-month intervention and follow-up; exercise training vs usual care  
**Why chosen:** It is a recent, adequately sized RCT in HFpEF, a population under-represented in earlier trials, and it reports hospitalisations by arm.  
**Found in:** Europe PMC · downloaded via: PMC open-data (AWS)  
**DOI:** [10.1038/s41591-024-03342-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-03342-7)  
**File:** `101038s41591-024-03342-7_34eaf7cf.pdf`

### Key findings

- All-cause hospitalisations occurred in 62 patients (38.5%) in the exercise group and 54 patients (33.5%) in the usual-care group (P = 0.35).
- Cardiovascular hospitalisations: 20.5% vs 19.3% (P = 0.78). Time to all-cause death or cardiovascular hospitalisation did not differ (log-rank P > 0.99).
- The primary composite endpoint (modified Packer score) was not met (tau-b -0.073, P = 0.17). Peak VO2 and NYHA class improved.
- Adherence was low: 47.5% of patients averaged at least two sessions per week overall.

**Relevance and caveats:** This trial gives no evidence that exercise training reduces hospitalisation in stable HFpEF. Hospitalisations were a safety/secondary outcome, not the primary endpoint, so the trial was not powered for them. Participants had relatively mild disease (about 85% NYHA II), and low adherence may have diluted any effect.

## 5. Real-world multidisciplinary cardiac rehabilitation after acute systolic HF and HF rehospitalisation

**Title:** Outcomes With Multidisciplinary Cardiac Rehabilitation in Post-acute Systolic Heart Failure Patients-A Retrospective Propensity Score-Matched Study  
**Authors:** Chen SM, Wang LY, Liaw MY, Wu MK, Wu PJ, Wei CL, Chen AN, Su TL, Chang JK, Yang TH, Chen C, Cheng CI, Chen PC, Chen YL.  
**Source:** Frontiers in cardiovascular medicine  
**Study type:** Retrospective propensity-score-matched cohort, single centre in Taiwan; 518 matched patients (137 CR), median follow-up 3.04 years  
**Why chosen:** It adds long-term real-world data on HF-specific rehospitalisation after discharge, which complements the short-term RCT evidence.  
**Found in:** Europe PMC · downloaded via: PMC open-data (AWS)  
**DOI:** [10.3389/fcvm.2022.763217](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcvm.2022.763217)  
**File:** `103389fcvm2022763217_f444513d.pdf`

### Key findings

- Cardiac rehabilitation was not significantly associated with HF rehospitalisation (adjusted HR 0.911, 95% CI 0.670 to 1.239, p = 0.553).
- Cardiac rehabilitation was associated with lower all-cause mortality (adjusted HR 0.490, 95% CI 0.308 to 0.778).
- Over follow-up, the HF rehospitalisation rate in the whole cohort was 34.6%.

**Relevance and caveats:** This study measures HF-specific readmission in a post-acute HFrEF population, but it is observational and single-centre. Residual confounding and healthy-adherer bias are likely. Cardiac rehabilitation was defined loosely (at least one exercise session within 3 months), which dilutes the exposure. The non-significant result does not show an absence of effect, because the CI is wide.
