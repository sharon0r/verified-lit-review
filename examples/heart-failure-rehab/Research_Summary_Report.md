# Literature Review Report (5 articles)

**Research question:** Does exercise-based cardiac rehabilitation reduce hospital readmissions in patients with heart failure?

**PICO:** Population: adults with heart failure (HFrEF or HFpEF, chronic or post-acute). Intervention: exercise-based cardiac rehabilitation (centre-, home- or hybrid-based exercise training, alone or in a comprehensive programme). Comparison: usual care / no exercise (or no CR participation or referral in observational studies). Outcome: hospital admissions or readmissions (all-cause and heart failure-related).

## Conclusion: what the literature says

The best randomized evidence (Cochrane 2024; 60 trials, 8728 participants) shows that exercise-based cardiac rehabilitation probably reduces all-cause hospital admissions within 12 months. The risk ratio was 0.69 (95% CI 0.56 to 0.86), with 15.9% admitted versus 23.8% in the control group (moderate certainty). Effects on heart failure-specific admissions (RR 0.82, 95% CI 0.49 to 1.35; 5.6% vs 6.4%) and on long-term all-cause admissions (RR 0.84, 95% CI 0.70 to 1.01) favour rehabilitation but are not statistically significant, so any benefit there is uncertain. The results are not fully consistent. A 2025 RCT of 322 stable patients with HFpEF found no significant difference in hospitalizations (38.5% vs 33.5% of patients; this was a safety outcome, not a primary endpoint). A Taiwanese propensity-matched cohort of patients with HFrEF also found no significant association with heart failure rehospitalization (adjusted HR 0.911, 95% CI 0.670–1.239). In contrast, a Danish national registry (33,257 patients) linked referral to rehabilitation with slightly lower odds of acute all-cause readmission (adjusted OR 0.92, 95% CI 0.87–0.97), though this may be confounded. A network meta-analysis suggests hybrid aerobic-plus-resistance programmes may perform best for readmissions, but it rests on only 7 trials and probably overlaps with the Cochrane review. Gaps remain for HFpEF, older and frail patients, patients recently hospitalized with acute decompensation, and heart failure-specific readmission.

_Numbers checked against the paper: 27/27 found_ ✅

**Certainty of evidence (GRADE-style, approximate):** Moderate for short-term (≤12 months) all-cause hospital admission. This rests mainly on the 2024 Cochrane review's GRADE rating: randomized evidence, downgraded once for risk of bias (poorly reported randomization and allocation concealment, lack of blinding). Heart failure-related admission: the Cochrane review also rates it moderate (downgraded for suspected publication bias), but its pooled 95% CI (0.49 to 1.35) includes 1. On our reading, imprecision would justify low certainty for this outcome. Long-term all-cause admission was downgraded for inconsistency (I2 = 61%), and its CI also includes 1. This is an approximate rating based on five open-access papers.

## 1. Pooled RCT evidence on all-cause and heart failure-related hospital admissions

**Title:** Exercise-based cardiac rehabilitation for adults with heart failure  
**Authors:** Molloy C, Long L, Mordi IR, Bridges C, Sagar VA, Davies EJ, Coats AJ, Dalal H  
**Source:** The Cochrane database of systematic reviews  
**Study type:** Cochrane systematic review and meta-analysis; 60 RCTs, 8728 participants; median follow-up 6 months  
**Why chosen:** It is the most recent and largest Cochrane synthesis of RCTs that directly addresses the question, and it uses GRADE.  
**Found in:** PubMed · downloaded via: OpenAlex  
**DOI:** [10.1002/14651858.CD003331.pub6](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD003331.pub6)  
**File:** `10100214651858CD003331pub6_721714be.pdf`

### Key findings

- Short-term (≤12 months) all-cause hospital admissions were lower with exercise-based rehabilitation: RR 0.69 (95% CI 0.56 to 0.86), 15.9% vs 23.8%; 23 trials, 2283 participants; moderate certainty.
- Short-term heart failure-related admissions: RR 0.82 (95% CI 0.49 to 1.35), 5.6% vs 6.4%; 10 trials, 911 participants. The CI includes 1, so the reduction was not statistically significant, although the authors rated it moderate certainty.
- Long-term (>12 months) all-cause admissions: RR 0.84 (95% CI 0.70 to 1.01); 7 trials, 3509 participants; I2 = 61%. Not statistically significant (P = 0.06).
- Effects appeared consistent across delivery modes (centre- vs home-based) and exercise dose. The Egger test suggested funnel-plot asymmetry for heart failure-related admission (P = 0.015).

_Numbers checked against the paper: 25/25 found_ ✅

**Relevance and caveats:** This review answers the question most directly with randomized evidence. Limits: most trials enrolled patients with HFrEF in NYHA class II–III; only two trials included patients with acute decompensated heart failure; randomization and allocation concealment were often poorly reported; and possible publication bias affects the heart failure admission outcome. The authors describe heart failure admissions as 'likely reduced' even though the CI includes 1, so this should be read cautiously.

## 2. Comparison of rehabilitation delivery modes (centre, home, hybrid; exercise types) on readmission

**Title:** Effects of exercise based cardiac rehabilitation delivery modes on chronic heart failure: a systematic review and network meta-analysis  
**Authors:** Hua C, Huang W, Chen Z, Cao D, Jia J, Chen X, Yang J, Zhang L  
**Source:** Scientific reports  
**Study type:** Systematic review and network meta-analysis; 33 RCTs, 5,900 participants with chronic heart failure; readmission reported in 7 trials  
**Why chosen:** It is the only recent synthesis comparing delivery modes and exercise types, which adds to the Cochrane review's comparison of exercise versus control.  
**Found in:** PubMed · downloaded via: PMC open-data (AWS)  
**DOI:** [10.1038/s41598-024-82608-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-82608-z)  
**File:** `101038s41598-024-82608-z_430b6b65.pdf`

### Key findings

- Readmission was analysed in only 7 two-arm trials covering six intervention strategies.
- Hybrid rehabilitation combining aerobic and resistance exercise ranked best for reducing readmission (SUCRA = 96%), and its 95% CI excluded 1.
- For the other pairwise comparisons on readmission, the 95% CIs included 1, so they showed no statistically significant differences.
- The authors note some funnel-plot asymmetry, which suggests possible publication bias.

_Numbers checked against the paper: 4/4 found_ ✅

**Relevance and caveats:** This paper is indirect support for the main question. Its readmission network is sparse (7 trials), and the paper does not report the pooled effect size for readmission in the main text, only the SUCRA ranking. SUCRA rankings are unstable when evidence is sparse. Its included trials probably overlap with the Cochrane review, so it is not independent confirmation.

### Quotes from the article

> "HCR (AE + RE) was the most effective intervention for reducing readmission rates among all treat­ments (SUCRA = 96%)."
>

## 3. Recent RCT of exercise training in HFpEF: hospitalizations

**Title:** Combined endurance and resistance exercise training in heart failure with preserved ejection fraction: a randomized controlled trial  
**Authors:** Edelmann F, Wachter R, Duvinage A, Mueller S, Fegers-Wustrow I, Schwarz S, Christle JW, Pieske-Kraigher E, Seyfarth M, Knapp M, Dörr M, Nolte K, Düngen HD, Herrmann-Lingen C, Esefeld K, Hagendorff A, Haykowsky MJ, Hasenfuss G, Holzendorf V, Prettin C, Mende M, Pieske B, Halle M.  
**Source:** Nature medicine  
**Study type:** Multicentre RCT; 322 patients with HFpEF (mean age 70 years, 59.6% women); 12 months of combined endurance and resistance training vs usual care  
**Why chosen:** It is a recent, well-reported RCT in HFpEF, an under-represented population, and it reports hospitalizations.  
**Found in:** Europe PMC · downloaded via: PMC open-data (AWS)  
**DOI:** [10.1038/s41591-024-03342-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-03342-7)  
**File:** `101038s41591-024-03342-7_34eaf7cf.pdf`

### Key findings

- All-cause hospitalizations (176 events in total) occurred in 62 patients (38.5%) in the exercise group and 54 patients (33.5%) in the usual-care group (P = 0.35). This difference was not statistically significant.
- Cardiovascular hospitalizations occurred in 20.5% vs 19.3% of patients (P = 0.78). Time to all-cause death or cardiovascular hospitalization did not differ significantly (log-rank P > 0.99).
- The primary endpoint (modified Packer score) was not met (Kendall's tau-b = -0.073, P = 0.17). Adherence was low: 38.1% of patients averaged at least two supervised sessions per week.

_Numbers checked against the paper: 17/17 found_ ✅

**Relevance and caveats:** This trial provides direct randomized data in HFpEF, but hospitalizations were reported as safety and secondary outcomes, and the trial was not powered for them. Patients were stable and mostly NYHA class II, and low adherence dilutes the effect. The results do not show a reduction in hospitalizations in this population and do not rule one out. Published in 2025, it is probably not included in the 2024 Cochrane review.

### Quotes from the article

## 4. Real-world post-acute HFrEF: rehabilitation participation and heart failure rehospitalization

**Title:** Outcomes With Multidisciplinary Cardiac Rehabilitation in Post-acute Systolic Heart Failure Patients-A Retrospective Propensity Score-Matched Study  
**Authors:** Chen SM, Wang LY, Liaw MY, Wu MK, Wu PJ, Wei CL, Chen AN, Su TL, Chang JK, Yang TH, Chen C, Cheng CI, Chen PC, Chen YL.  
**Source:** Frontiers in cardiovascular medicine  
**Study type:** Retrospective propensity-score-matched cohort, single centre in Taiwan; 518 matched patients (137 in the rehabilitation group); median follow-up 3.04 years  
**Why chosen:** It is an observational study of patients after acute HFrEF that uses propensity matching and reports heart failure rehospitalization separately.  
**Found in:** Europe PMC · downloaded via: PMC open-data (AWS)  
**DOI:** [10.3389/fcvm.2022.763217](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcvm.2022.763217)  
**File:** `103389fcvm2022763217_f444513d.pdf`

### Key findings

- Rehabilitation participation was not significantly associated with heart failure rehospitalization: adjusted HR 0.911 (95% CI 0.670–1.239), p = 0.553.
- Participation was associated with lower all-cause mortality: adjusted HR 0.490 (95% CI 0.308–0.778).
- The heart failure rehospitalization rate was 34.6% over a median of 3.04 years.

_Numbers checked against the paper: 12/12 found_ ✅

**Relevance and caveats:** This is directly relevant for post-acute HFrEF, but the design is observational: there may be residual confounding and healthy-adherer bias, and the 'CR group' needed only one exercise session. It is a single-centre study with small numbers. The non-significant result for rehospitalization does not show that rehabilitation has no effect.

### Quotes from the article

> "Likewise, the association between the rate of HF rehospitalization and CR was not signiﬁcant (HR: 0.911 [95% CI: 0.670–1.239], p = 0.553) in this study."

## 5. National registry: rehabilitation referral and acute all-cause readmission

**Title:** Cardiac rehabilitation for patients with heart failure: association with readmission and mortality risk  
**Authors:** Lau Caspar Thygesen, Line Zinckernagel, Hasnain M. Dalal, Kenneth Egstrup, Charlotte Glümer, Morten Grønbæk, Teresa Holmberg, Lars Køber  
**Source:** European Heart Journal - Quality of Care and Clinical Outcomes  
**Study type:** Nationwide registry cohort (Danish Heart Failure Registry); 33,257 patients with incident heart failure, 2010–2018; 1-year outcomes; multivariable logistic regression  
**Why chosen:** It is the largest real-world population in the candidate set and reports readmission directly.  
**Found in:** OpenAlex · downloaded via: search result link  
**DOI:** [10.1093/ehjqcco/qcab086](https://doi.org/10.1093/ehjqcco/qcab086)  
**File:** `101093ehjqccoqcab086_32912207.pdf`

### Key findings

- Referral to cardiac rehabilitation was associated with lower odds of acute all-cause readmission at 1 year: adjusted OR 0.92 (95% CI 0.87–0.97).
- Referral was also associated with lower all-cause mortality (OR 0.65; 0.58–0.72).
- Only 46.7% of patients were referred, and those not referred were older, more often living alone, had more comorbidities and were more often NYHA class IV, which points to strong selection.

_Numbers checked against the paper: 9/9 found_ ✅

**Relevance and caveats:** The exposure is referral rather than actual exercise participation, so the study is indirect. The design is observational, with likely confounding by indication and frailty. The association with readmission is small (OR 0.92), and the study cannot establish causation.
